02What “privacy-first” means here, precisely

Vague promises are worthless, so here is the exact shape of it.

  1. A visitor's IP address is never read. Not logged, not hashed, not counted. There is no RemoteAddr in the codebase.
  2. The User-Agent is inspected in flight when a link has device-specific destinations, and never stored.
  3. No cookies for visitors. A cookie is only ever set for someone signing in to the dashboard.
  4. Counters are aggregate: total clicks and the timestamp of the last one. Nothing that can reconstruct who went where.
  5. Tracking parameters are stripped, and only those: utm_*, fbclid, gclid, msclkid, mc_eid and their kind go; id=7 stays.

03What it does

Everything a shortener is for, and nothing that needs to know who you are.

Strips the tracking, keeps the link working

Removing query parameters is easy. Removing only the ones that track you, and leaving the ones the page needs, is the whole job. Checked as you paste, before anything is stored.

Nobody is counted twice

No IP address is read. Counters are aggregate: total clicks and when the last one happened. Nothing that reconstructs who went where.

Routes by device, not by person

One link, an App Store target for iOS and a Play Store target for Android, or an A/B split. The User-Agent is read in flight to decide, and never stored.

Links that end themselves

A PIN in front, an expiry date, or a click budget after which the link self-destructs. Unlock attempts are limited per link, not per visitor, because there is no visitor to limit.

Your own domains

Point a domain you own at it and short links come out under your name. Several at once, if you keep projects apart.

Made to be scripted

API keys for a CLI, a bot or an editor plugin, bulk import from CSV, export back out, and HMAC-signed webhooks when a link is created or runs out.
