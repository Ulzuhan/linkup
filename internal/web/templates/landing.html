How it works

Three steps, and nobody gets followed

  1. 1

    Paste the link

    Any URL. The tracking parameters are struck out as you type, so you see what will be stored before it is.

  2. 2

    Set how it behaves

    A custom slug, your own domain, a PIN in front, an expiry date or a click budget. Or none of it: one click and it exists.

  3. 3

    Share it

    Copy it, or show a QR code drawn right here. Whoever clicks is counted, never identified: no address, no cookie, no fingerprint.

What “privacy-first” means here, precisely

Everything a shortener is for, and nothing that needs to know who you are

  • Strips the tracking, keeps the link working

    Removing query parameters is easy. Removing only the ones that track you, and leaving the ones the page needs, is the whole job. utm_*, fbclid, gclid go; id=7 stays.

  • A visitor's address is never read

    Not logged, not hashed, not counted. Counters are aggregate: total clicks and when the last one happened. Nothing that reconstructs who went where.

  • Routes by device, not by person

    One link, an App Store target for iOS and a Play Store target for Android, or an A/B split. The User-Agent is read in flight to decide, and never stored.

  • Links that end themselves

    A PIN in front, an expiry date, or a click budget after which the link self-destructs. Unlock attempts are limited per link, not per visitor, because there is no visitor to limit.

  • Your own domains

    Point a domain you own at it and short links come out under your name. Several at once, if you keep projects apart. No cookies for visitors on any of them.

  • Made to be scripted

    API keys for a CLI, a bot or an editor plugin, bulk import from CSV, export back out, and HMAC-signed webhooks when a link is created or runs out.
